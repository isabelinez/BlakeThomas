Earn Every Increase
4-WEEK PROGRESSION
Week 1 · Learn
Technique first
  • Find comfortable, controlled ranges of motion on every exercise.
  • Learn the technique and the bracing and breathing pattern.
  • Pick manageable starting weights. When in doubt, go lighter.
Week 2 · Build Consistency
Same plan, cleaner reps
  • Same exercises and same technique.
  • Aim to complete the prescribed reps with controlled effort at RPE 6–7.
Week 3 · Progress Selectively
Only where it's earned
  • If you completed all reps with consistent technique, had no concerning symptoms, and recovered well, add a rep or a small amount of weight.
  • Don't increase everything at once. Pick the exercises that are ready.
Week 4 · Consolidate
Lock it in
  • Hold or modestly improve your numbers.
  • No forced personal records and no extra volume just because it's the final week.
  • Complete the end-of-phase reassessment on the Progress tab.
Double Progression
01
Reps First
Start with a weight you can lift for the bottom of the rep range at RPE 6–7. Each session, try to add a rep or two while your form stays the same.
02
Then a Small Weight Increase
When you hit the top of the range on every set, with clean reps and RPE 7 or lower, add the smallest jump available next session: about 2.5–5 lb per dumbbell for upper body, 5–10 lb on lower-body machines. Then drop back to the bottom of the range and build up again.
Example · Dumbbell Bench 3 × 8–10
  • 40s for 10, 9, 8: stay at 40s.
  • 40s for 10, 10, 9: stay at 40s.
  • 40s for 10, 10, 10 at RPE 7: move to 45s next session and aim for 8+ on every set.
Decision Rules
Hold the Weight
  • You hit the reps, but effort was RPE 8 or higher.
  • The last reps looked different from the first ones.
  • Sleep, stress, or soreness made it a rough week.
  • It's a new or recently regressed exercise.
Reduce the Weight (~10%)
  • Reps drop below the range.
  • Form breaks down before the set ends.
  • The set feels like RPE 9.
  • Your back feels off or tight during the lift, without pain.
Regress the Exercise
  • You can't keep consistent technique even with a light load.
  • A position or range is uncomfortable. Use the listed alternative or a shorter range.
Stop the Movement
  • Pain, not just effort or muscle burn.
  • Symptoms travelling into the buttock or leg, numbness, tingling, or sharp discomfort.
  • Switch to a comfortable alternative, tell Isa, and check the Back Care tab.
Hip-Hinge Ladder
Deadlift & RDL Progression
Move up one level after 2 sessions in a row with clean reps and no symptoms during or over the next 24 hrs
  • Level 1: Dowel hip hinge. Hips back, three contact points stay on the dowel.
  • Level 2: Hip-to-wall hinge. Move your feet farther from the wall as control improves.
  • Level 3: Kettlebell deadlift from a raised start (step or plates), light load.
  • Level 4: Kettlebell deadlift from the floor. Lower the start height before adding weight.
  • Level 5: Dumbbell RDL, short range, stopping around knee height.
  • Level 6: Dumbbell RDL through your full comfortable range.
Easy, Steady, Low-Impact
CARDIO PROGRAM
How Hard: RPE 3–4 out of 10
  • Breathing a little faster, but you can still hold a conversation.
  • If you can only get a few words out, slow down.
  • No intervals required in this phase.
Weeks 1–2
  • 2–3 sessions per week
  • 15–20 minutes each
Weeks 3–4
  • 2–3 sessions per week
  • 20–25 minutes each, if you're sleeping well and recovering from lifting
What to Use
  • Treadmill walk at a comfortable speed. Incline is optional; only add it if it feels good on your back and legs.
  • Stationary bike, upright or recumbent
  • Elliptical or another low-impact option you enjoy
Fitting It Around Lifting
  • Best slots: after Monday or Thursday upper-body sessions, Wednesday, and Saturday.
  • Do cardio after lifting, not before, so you're fresh for the weights.
  • Avoid long or hard cardio the day before a lower-body session.
  • Sore or tired? Keep it short and easy, or skip it.
Sample Week
  • Monday: easy cardio after lifting.
  • Wednesday: easy walk (active recovery).
  • Saturday: easy bike or walk plus mobility.

RECOVERY PROGRAM
😴
Sleep 7–9 Hours
Keep a consistent bedtime and wake time. Cut caffeine about 8 hours before bed. Dark, cool room, and put your phone away for the last 30 minutes.
🚶
Wednesday · Active Recovery
Optional 20–30 min easy walk plus 5–10 min of comfortable mobility from your cooldowns. Nothing hard.
🚲
Saturday · Optional Easy Cardio
Easy cardio and gentle mobility only. It's not another workout.
📆
Sunday · Full Rest
Normal daily activity is great. No planned training.
💧
Hydrate
Drink before, during, and after training, especially in the Florida heat. See the Nutrition tab for your daily target.
🔥
Soreness Is Normal
Muscle soreness 24–72 hours after a session is expected, especially in weeks 1–2. Light movement helps. Pain in a joint or your lower back that is sharp, spreads, or lingers is different. Tell Isa.
When to Back Off
01
Watch for These Signs
Under 6 hours of sleep for 2+ nights. Soreness lasting beyond 72 hours or changing how you move. Performance dropping two sessions in a row. Feeling unusually run down or getting sick.
02
How to Back Off
Drop one set per exercise, use lighter weights (RPE 5–6), and skip or shorten cardio. Return to the normal plan once you feel recovered.
03
Consistency Beats Intensity
Showing up four times a week at RPE 6–7 builds more than occasional all-out sessions. No sets to failure in this phase.
Simple Habits · No Food Scale Required
NUTRITION PROGRAM
🎯 The Strategy
  • A moderate calorie deficit drives fat loss. No crash diets.
  • Enough protein plus lifting helps you keep and build muscle while you lose fat.
  • No foods are banned. Build balanced plates most of the time and enjoy favorites on purpose.
✅ Daily Check
Tap when you hit your protein target and fill a cup for each ~8 oz of water.
Protein
🥩 Protein · 120–150g Daily
  • Spread across 3–4 meals, about 30–40g each.
  • A palm-sized portion of meat or fish is roughly 20–30g.
  • Lead every meal and snack with protein.

🛒 Easy Protein Options
  • Chicken, turkey, lean beef, pork loin, fish, shrimp
  • Eggs and egg whites
  • Greek yogurt, cottage cheese

  • Beans, lentils, tofu, edamame
🍳 Simple Meal Ideas
  • Breakfast: eggs plus egg whites, toast, fruit. Or Greek yogurt, berries, granola.
  • Lunch: chicken rice bowl with veggies and salsa, or a turkey wrap and side salad.
  • Dinner: salmon or lean steak, potatoes, roasted vegetables.
  • Snacks: protein shake, cottage cheese and fruit, jerky, apple and string cheese.
💧 Hydration
  • Start around 80–100 oz a day; more on training days and in hot weather.
  • Urine should mostly be pale yellow.
  • On very sweaty days, an electrolyte drink can help.
  • With a heart, kidney, or other condition that affects fluid needs, follow your doctor's guidance.
🥤 Drinks & Snacking
  • Swap regular soda, sweet tea, juice, and specialty coffees for zero-calorie or lighter versions most days.
  • Alcohol adds calories and hurts sleep. Set a weekly limit you can stick to.
  • Plan your snacks instead of grazing.
  • Keep favorite foods in, in a portion you decide ahead of time.
⚖️ The Scale
  • Realistic rate: about 0.75–1.5 lb per week. 165 lb is a goal for the coming months, not these 4 weeks.
  • Weigh in the morning after the bathroom, before eating, 3–7 times a week.
  • Compare weekly averages, not single days. Daily numbers move with water, salt, and carbs.
  • If your weekly average stalls for 2–3 weeks, Isa will adjust portions.
Medical Note
  • If you have kidney disease, another relevant medical condition, or diet restrictions from a clinician, get individualized guidance before using these general targets.
Train Smart, Not Scared
BACK CARE
You're not injured, and you're not training like you are. You've had back pain before, so we start conservatively, watch how you respond, and build from there.
Effort vs. a Warning Sign
  • Normal: muscles working hard, a pump, soreness in the muscles you trained over the next day or two.
  • Tell Isa: back pain that is sharp, spreads, catches during a lift, or feels worse the next morning than normal soreness.
The 24-Hour Check

If a movement feels fine during the session and your back feels normal the next day, keep progressing as planned. If your back feels worse the next day, hold or adjust that movement.

If Back Pain Comes Back
01
Stop or Modify
Stop the exercise that provoked it, or change the load or range.
02
Reassess
Check the load, range of motion, setup, and technique.
03
Use a Comfortable Alternative
Every exercise lists one under "How to do it." No pushing through pain.
04
Get It Checked If It Sticks Around
See a qualified healthcare professional if pain persists, keeps coming back, limits daily activities, or concerns you.
Get Urgent Medical Attention
  • New bowel or bladder control problems.
  • Numbness around the groin or saddle area.
  • Leg weakness that's getting worse.
  • Severe pain after a fall or accident, or back pain with fever, unexplained weight loss, or severe pain at night, also needs prompt medical evaluation.
